# Enrollment-Trend-Analysis

**Project Overview**
---

This project examined enrollment and academic performance trends among transfer students to better understand how demographic and academic factors influenced student outcomes over time. Using historical enrollment and performance data, I identified patterns across transfer student populations, analyzed equity-related outcomes among historically marginalized groups, and developed projections to support institutional planning and enrollment policy decisions.

**Question:**
---

What enrollment and academic performance patterns emerged over time, and what might those patterns suggest about future enrollment outcomes for historically marginalized student populations?

**Project Objectives**
---

- Analyze historical enrollment and academic performance trends among transfer student populations.
- Identify demographic differences associated with academic outcomes.
- Evaluate relationships between academic performance, transfer shock, and enrollment persistence.
- Assess potential impacts of policy changes on historically marginalized student populations.
- Develop data-informed projections to support strategic enrollment planning.

**Methodology**
---

**Data Collection**

- Reviewed historical enrollment (over 3-year period), transfer, and academic performance data.
- Validated student-level records and standardized demographic and academic variables.
- Segmented populations into Program (P) and Non-Program (NP) transfer student cohorts.
- Organized data to support demographic and longitudinal trend analysis.

**Data Analysis**

- Conducted historical trend analysis to identify changes in enrollment and performance outcomes over time.
- Examined relationships between transfer GPA, first-fall GPA, cumulative GPA, and enrollment outcomes.
- Evaluated demographic representation across academic performance categories.
- Analyzed transfer shock trends among student populations.

**Reporting and Visualization**

- Developed comprehensive reports highlighting performance trends, demographic patterns, and enrollment outcomes.

**Tools Used**
---

- Excel
- Tableau
- Power BI

**Key Findings**
---

- Identified differences in academic performance and enrollment outcomes among Program (P) and Non-Program (NP) transfer students, including variations in transfer GPA, first-fall GPA, and cumulative GPA.
- Found notable demographic differences across academic performance levels. As GPA increased, Black/African American representation decreased by approximately 61% among P students and 47% among NP students, while Hispanic/Latino representation increased among higher-performing students.
- Identified gender-related performance patterns, with female student representation increasing by approximately 26% for P students and 29% for NP students as GPA increased.
- Found that higher transfer GPAs were associated with increased transfer shock, rising approximately 33% among P students and 54% among NP students.
- Identified a relationship between higher academic performance and improved enrollment outcomes, with ineligible registration statuses decreasing as GPA increased.

**Business Recommendations**
---

- Evaluate proposed admission policy changes using demographic impact analyses to ensure requirements do not unintentionally reduce access for historically marginalized student populations.
- Develop targeted support resources addressing transfer shock, including academic coaching, faculty mentoring, and transition-focused programming during students' first academic year.
- Regularly examine demographic representation across academic performance outcomes to monitor equity gaps and measure progress toward institutional student success goals.
- Incorporate trend analysis and enrollment forecasting into strategic planning efforts to anticipate future enrollment shifts and support informed policy decisions.

**Analytical Limitations**
---

- The analysis identified relationships between demographic characteristics, academic performance, and enrollment outcomes but **did not establish direct causation**.
- Observed performance differences may reflect the influence of factors not included in the analysis, such as financial need, work obligations, transfer institution characteristics, campus engagement, or access to academic support resources.
- Historical trends may not fully predict future enrollment behavior because institutional policies, economic conditions, and student demographics can change over time.
- Measures of transfer shock captured changes in academic performance following transfer but did not identify the specific factors contributing to those changes.
- Because the analysis relied on historical institutional data, some student experiences and contextual variables could not be directly measured.

**Conclusion**
---

This analysis identified meaningful relationships between demographic characteristics, academic performance, transfer shock, and enrollment outcomes among transfer students. The findings revealed important equity considerations, including differences in representation across academic performance levels and the potential impact of enrollment policies on historically marginalized student populations.

The project provided institutional leadership with evidence-based insights to support enrollment planning, retention initiatives, and admission policy discussions. Most notably, the analysis informed leadership's decision not to increase admission GPA requirements due to concerns that the change could disproportionately affect access and enrollment opportunities for underrepresented student groups. By combining trend analysis, demographic evaluation, and enrollment forecasting, the project demonstrated how analytics can support more equitable and data-informed institutional decision-making.
